JD · Senior Fellow, The Conference Board · Adjunct Professor, NYU Stern

A lawyer who spent his career on a question most organisations would rather not be asked out loud: what actually makes one behave honestly?

He has put it to students in New York, France, Portugal, Argentina and China.

The through-line

Ethics programmes are easy to announce
and hard to mean.

He joined The Conference Board in 1977 and started its ethics programme, then spent decades expanding it, at a time when corporate ethics was more often a statement than a function.

In 2010 the Board made him a Senior Fellow. By then the same question had followed him into a lecture hall at NYU Stern, where he has taught as an Adjunct Professor since 1995.

Where he taught it

The same subject,
five rooms, four continents.

Business ethics does not translate cleanly. What counts as a conflict of interest, a gift, or an ordinary courtesy shifts the moment you cross a border. That is the argument for teaching it in more than one.
